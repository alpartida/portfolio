## Context

sltrib.com runs on Arc XP Fusion — the publishing platform the Washington Post
built and now licenses to other newsrooms. Work happens in React components
that slot into editor-configurable page layouts, backed by Node services and
Lambda functions. I've contributed around 850 commits to it since 2021.

The Tribune converted to nonprofit status, which shapes the engineering
priorities: the features that keep the lights on are newsletters,
subscriptions and donations, and they get the same care as the editorial tools.

## Landing pages

A steady part of the job is landing pages — standalone pages built for a
specific campaign, offer or project, outside the article templates the
newsroom publishes into every day. They arrive with a real date attached and
people waiting on them, so they are good practice at building something
complete and correct quickly, rather than iterating on it for a quarter.

## Migrating the newsletter platform

One of the largest pieces of work was moving the Tribune's newsletter system
from Mailchimp to Iterable. That is less a swap than a rebuild:

- New signup components across the site, including the inline units that appear
  inside articles
- A reader-facing preferences page where subscribers choose which newsletters
  they get
- The Lambda functions behind it — subscribe, unsubscribe, and update
  preferences — talking to Iterable's API
- Retiring every Mailchimp-connected component once traffic had moved over

Newsletter signup is a conversion surface, so the work had to be correct on the
first try in production: a broken unsubscribe link is both a bad reader
experience and a compliance problem.

## Revenue features

A nonprofit newsroom asks readers for money constantly, and how you ask matters.
I built and maintain:

- Banner offers targeted to article pages
- The donation ask, including copy and layout changes the audience team could run
- Gift subscriptions, surfaced in the footer and the subscribe flow
- Article sponsorship components, including sponsored coverage of the Utah Jazz

## The bill tracker

Utah's legislative session is a major story every year. I worked on the bill
tracker that lets readers follow legislation — a sortable table fed by a
bills API, including the null-safety work that keeps a table rendering when
upstream data is incomplete, which with government APIs is often.

## Platform work

Alongside features: upgrading the application to React 18, an Airtable
migration, environment and secret configuration across sandbox and production,
and caching work. The unglamorous half of a long-lived codebase, and the half
that decides whether the next three years of feature work is pleasant.

## What it teaches

Shipping continuously to an audience that notices immediately when something
breaks. It's also where I learned to work inside someone else's platform — you
don't get to rewrite Arc XP, so you learn its grain and build with it. That
habit carries straight into client work, where the constraint is usually a
budget or a non-technical owner rather than a CMS.
