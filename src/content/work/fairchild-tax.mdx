## The problem

A tax practice has a brutally seasonal business and a narrow set of questions
it gets asked over and over: what do you charge, what do I need to bring, where
are you, are you taking new clients this year. Every one of those answered on
the phone is time not spent on returns.

The site needed to answer them before anyone picks up the phone — and it needed
a way to post a notice at the top during filing season without waiting on me.

## The approach

One page, in the order a prospective client actually needs it:

- **A notice slot at the top** for time-sensitive messages — deadline changes,
  whether the practice is accepting new clients, holiday hours. This is the part
  of the site that changes most and it's deliberately the easiest thing to edit.
- **"How much does it cost?"** as a real section rather than a "call for a
  quote." Publishing pricing filters out mismatched enquiries before they become
  phone calls.
- **"At your service"** — what the practice actually does, in plain terms.
- **A map and contact details**, because a local practice lives and dies on
  people being able to find it. The Google Maps embed is pinned to the business
  listing rather than a dropped pin, so it matches what people see in search.
- **A contact form** that sends an enquiry straight through, with the fields
  needed to triage it.

## On being honest about the build

This one started from a Bootstrap template rather than from scratch, and that
was the right call. The client needed a credible, working site inside a tax
season, on a small budget. Starting from a solid template and spending the time
on content, pricing clarity, mobile layout and the maintenance story served
them better than a bespoke build would have at that price.

Knowing when a project doesn't need a custom front end is part of the job.
